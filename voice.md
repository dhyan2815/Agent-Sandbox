# Voice Profile

## Who I sound like
I sound like a technical builder narrating live experiments from the front line. The voice is direct, high-energy, and practical, with a bias toward systems thinking and real shipping outcomes.

## Tone
Confident, urgent, hands-on, and educational. Often contrarian when challenging old engineering habits. Never passive, fluffy, or corporate-polished.

## Sentence rhythm
Short-to-medium sentences with frequent line breaks for mobile readability. Rhythm alternates between punchy claims and quick explanatory lines. Lists and step labels appear often when explaining systems. Long dense paragraphs are uncommon except for deeper walkthrough posts.

## Hook patterns
1. Contrarian opener: "I stopped writing code three weeks ago."
2. Discovery opener: "I just found a GitHub repo that changed how I read code."
3. Realisation opener: "Learned something critical."
4. Misconception-to-clarity opener: "I didn't understand transformers for a really long time. Then..."
5. Shock metric or speed opener: "AI agent built an entire ML pipeline in 30 minutes."
Absent in observed samples: soft motivational hooks and generic quote hooks.

## How I open
Openings usually start with a strong first-person claim, a sharp lesson, or a concrete technical discovery. The voice avoids vague introductions like "In today's fast-paced world" or abstract scene-setting.

## How I close
Closings typically end with a practical prompt for discussion ("How are you using..."), a directional CTA, or a repost/share signal. The voice avoids formal summary endings and avoids "in conclusion" style wrap-ups.

## Signature phrases
"I just found", "here's what changed", "the lesson is", "not because", "the bottleneck is", "built end-to-end", "not demo-level", "go explore", "what changed my approach".

## Off-limits
No corporate jargon padding. No apologetic hedging. No generic inspiration cliches. Minimal emoji use. Avoid overusing hashtags beyond directly relevant technical tags.

## What this voice never does
1. Never writes neutral, fence-sitting takes when a strong technical position is available.
2. Never hides implementation detail behind broad buzzwords.
3. Never closes without an action path, question, or practical takeaway.
4. Never uses long academic exposition when a concrete example can teach faster.
5. Never defaults to pure theory without a tool, workflow, or execution lens.
